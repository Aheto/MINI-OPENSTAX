Noun Pronoun Common Proper Abstract Collective Antecedent Agreement

📘 Understanding Nouns & Pronouns

Language works like a puzzle. Nouns and pronouns are the foundational pieces that help us name people, places, things, and ideas clearly.

📦 Types of Nouns

  • Common Nouns: General names (e.g., teacher, market, book)
  • Proper Nouns: Specific names, always capitalized (e.g., Ama, Kumasi, NaCCA)
  • Abstract Nouns: Ideas, qualities, or feelings (e.g., honesty, bravery, education)
  • Collective Nouns: Groups treated as one unit (e.g., team, class, flock)

🔄 What are Pronouns?

Pronouns replace nouns to avoid repetition. The noun being replaced is called the antecedent.

💬 "Kofi bought a new bicycle because Kofi wanted to exercise." → "Kofi bought a new bicycle because he wanted to exercise."

📋 Pronoun Categories

TypeExamplesUsage
PersonalI, you, he, she, it, we, theyRefer to specific people/things
Possessivemy, your, his, her, its, our, theirShow ownership
Demonstrativethis, that, these, thosePoint to specific items

⚠️ Pronoun-Antecedent Agreement

A pronoun must match its antecedent in number (singular/plural) and gender (he/she/they).

  • ❌ Every student must bring their book. (Mismatch: singular student → plural their)
  • ✅ Every student must bring his or her book. OR All students must bring their books.

✅ Check Your Understanding

  1. Find three proper nouns in your classroom or community.
  2. Rewrite this sentence using a pronoun: "Ama and Esi went to the market because Ama and Esi needed tomatoes."
  3. Identify the abstract noun: "The team showed great courage during the match."
  4. Explain why pronouns make writing clearer and less repetitive.
